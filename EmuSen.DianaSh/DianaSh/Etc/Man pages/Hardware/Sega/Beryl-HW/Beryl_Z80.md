# Beryl_Z80 — the Z80's record

*Started 2026-10-04, at stage 0.* `beryl-z80`, the Zilog Z80 shared by Nephrite's sound CPU and, later, Endou's and
Jadeite's main CPU. Planned in `Nephrite_Plan.md` §8 and §6's stage 2; the common interface is `README.md` beside this
page. Registers as in `Nephrite_Native.md`.

## 1. Stage 0 (2026-10-04)

The bus trait, the registers and the step's signature (`NotBuilt` while `BUILT` is false), and the single-step harness
with its controls. No instruction is implemented.

## 2. The interface

- **`Bus`**: `fetch` (the M1 cycle's read, then the refresh with I and R on the address bus), `read`, `write`, `input`
  and `output` (the whole 16-bit port address, as the processor drives it), `idle` (T-states without a memory or I/O
  cycle), `int_line`, `nmi_edge` and `acknowledge` (the byte a device places on the bus during the interrupt
  acknowledge, mode 0's instruction or mode 2's vector; the bus counts the cycle's six T-states, its two automatic
  wait states included, §5). The bus adds WAIT's states: on the Genesis, the Z80's window
  onto the 68000's space costs both processors.
- **`Registers`**: the main and alternate pairs, IX, IY, SP, PC, I, R, WZ (MEMPTR), Q (whether the last instruction
  changed the flags, which SCF and CCF read), P (whether the last instruction was LD A,I or LD A,R, whose P/V an
  accepted interrupt changes), IFF1, IFF2, IM, and EI's one-instruction delay.

## 3. The oracle

### 3.1 The single-step harness

`src/singlestep.rs`, over SingleStepTests' `z80/v1` (1,604 files × 1,000 cases, every opcode with its prefixes; its
README says it was generated from a translation of another emulator's core, and its `generation/` folder is not read),
found through `EMUSEN_BERYL_CORPUS`.

**The bus** answers memory from the case's list and port reads from the case's `ports` in order, and records each call
as the T-states the suite samples, expanded by the patterns the suite's data shows (statements about the file format,
not the hardware):

| Call | T-states (address, data, pins `rwmi`) |
|---|---|
| fetch | (pc, –, `----`), (pc, –, `r-m-`), (refresh, opcode, `----`), (refresh, –, `----`) |
| read | (a, –, `----`), (a, –, `r-m-`), (a, value, `----`) |
| write | (a, –, `----`), (a, value, `-wm-`), (a, –, `----`) |
| input | (p, –, `----`), (p, –, `----`), (p, –, `r--i`), (p, value, `----`) |
| output | (p, –, `----`), (p, –, `----`), (p, value, `-w-i`), (p, –, `----`) |
| idle | (any, –, `----`) per T-state |

The suite samples between T-states and pulses MREQ with RD or WR for one T-state (its README's "simplified memory
access" option); both are the generator's conventions, which the harness reproduces rather than judges. **An internal
T-state's address is not compared:** the suite's README says it records the last address driven "for convenience",
the address pins being disconnected; the Genesis's bus does not observe it. Each case is graded on the registers, the
memory, and the T-state list with the I/O transactions.

**The controls (measured 2026-10-04).**

- *Positive:* a replay that reads each case's T-states back into the bus calls that make them and performs them,
  ending in the case's registers: **1,604,000 of 1,604,000** pass, in 3.8 s on four threads.
- *Negative:* the carry flipped in the final F, and the last T-state dropped, over the first 200 cases of every eighth
  file: **0 of 40,200** pass for each.
- *The unbuilt processor* fails the inline case.

### 3.2 The hardware-grounded check

ZEXDOC and ZEXALL (`agn453/ZEXALL` @`8f71d41`), whose CRCs were taken on real Z80s, run at stage 2 in the crate under a
small CP/M shim (BDOS functions 2 and 9). They are the one Z80 oracle not generated by an emulator (`Nephrite_Plan.md`
§9, Q3).

## 4. Stage 2, step 1: the instruction set (2026-10-05)

### 4.1 What it built

`src/exec.rs`: every opcode with its prefixes, the undocumented ones included (the index registers' halves, SLL, the
DD CB and FD CB forms that also write a register, `IN (C)`, `OUT (C),0`, the ED NOPs), with WZ, Q and P. The operations
and their machine cycles are Zilog's Z80 CPU User Manual's; the undocumented opcodes and the X and Y flags are "The
Undocumented Z80 Documented" (Sean Young); WZ follows the published MEMPTR notes (boo_boo and Vladimir Kladov), Q and
the X and Y of SCF and CCF the published Q notes. Each machine cycle is one bus call (`fetch`, `read`, `write`, `input`,
`output`), and the extra T-states inside a machine cycle are `idle` calls placed where the manual's cycle lengths put
them: the fifth T-state of PUSH's M1, the fourth of CALL's second operand read, the five after JR's displacement, the
five before an indexed operand is read.

**Measured 2026-10-05, SingleStepTests' Z80 suite: 1,604,000 of 1,604,000 cases, registers, memory and every
T-state with its I/O.** The first build passed 1,603,115 (99.94%); the 885 it missed were one reading, the first of
§4.2. The suite runs in 3.2 s on four threads.

### 4.2 Readings beyond the documents

- **A DD or FD prefix leaves Q at zero.** SCF and CCF take X and Y from `(Q ^ F) | A`, Q being F if the last
  instruction wrote the flags and zero otherwise. The prefix is fetched as an instruction of its own that writes no
  flag, so SCF or CCF behind it read Q as zero. *Taken from the suite:* all 885 of the first build's misses, DD and FD
  with 37 and 3F.
- **A repeating block instruction** (LDIR, CPIR, INIR, OTIR and their decrementing forms), going round again, puts the
  PC back on itself, sets WZ one past it, and takes X and Y from bits 13 and 11 of its own address. A repeating I/O
  instruction also changes H and P/V by the carry of its count and B. These follow the measurements David Banks
  published in 2018 on real Z80s, which the manual and Young's document predate. The suite agrees in every case.
- **POP AF and EX AF,AF' leave Q at zero**: they load F rather than compute it. The suite agrees.
- **HALT** advances the PC past itself, as the suite records it; §5 gives what follows.

None of the oracles disagree with one another, so stage 2 needs no dispute step.

### 4.3 Provenance

Read to write this step: Zilog's Z80 CPU User Manual (UM0080), "The Undocumented Z80 Documented" (version 0.91), the
MEMPTR notes, the Q notes, Banks's block-instruction measurements, and the suite's data and README. No emulator source
was opened, the suite's `generation/` folder included.

## 5. Interrupts and HALT

At an instruction boundary, in this order: an NMI edge; INT, when IFF1 is set and the last instruction was not EI;
HALT's NOP; then the observer's stop and the instruction (`src/exec.rs`, `step`).

| Event | Bus activity | T-states |
|---|---|---|
| NMI | an M1 at the PC, its opcode ignored, and one internal T-state; the PC stacked, high byte first; the handler at $0066 | 11 |
| INT, mode 0 | the acknowledge cycle; the byte on the bus run as an instruction, an RST as its own five T-states less the fetch | 13 for an RST |
| INT, mode 1 | the acknowledge cycle, one internal T-state, the PC stacked; the handler at $0038 | 13 |
| INT, mode 2 | the acknowledge cycle, one internal T-state, the PC stacked, then the handler's address read from I and the vector | 19 |
| HALT | an M1 at the PC that does not advance it, R counting | 4 a step |

NMI clears IFF1 and keeps IFF2, which RETN copies back; INT clears both. An accepted interrupt ends HALT and sets WZ to
the handler's address. R counts the acknowledge cycle as an M1. An INT accepted right after LD A,I or LD A,R clears the
P/V that instruction set from IFF2, which is what P records (the NMOS Z80's known fault). Mode 0 runs a one-byte
instruction from the bus; an instruction with operands placed on the bus is not modelled, since the Genesis, the
Master System and the Game Gear all run mode 1. `src/boundary.rs` pins each against a bus that counts T-states. **These
are argued from the manual; the single-step suite has no interrupt case.** The machines' own interrupt tests are their
test.

## 6. The CP/M shim, the disassembler, the state and the cost

### 6.1 ZEXDOC and ZEXALL (measured 2026-10-05)

`src/cpm.rs`: a CP/M shim of two BDOS functions. The program goes at $0100, a RET at $0005 answers functions 2 (a
character) and 9 (a string ending in `$`), $0006 holds the top of memory, and a jump to $0000 ends the run. The
programs are found through `EMUSEN_BERYL_ZEX` (`agn453/ZEXALL` @`8f71d41`, copied at
`~/.cache/emusen/probe/nephrite/roms/zexall`). **ZEXDOC: 67 of 67 tests. ZEXALL: 67 of 67 tests**, each in
46,734,977,142 T-states and 47 s, about a billion T-states a second. Their CRCs were taken on real Z80s, so this is
the one Z80 oracle not generated by an emulator, and it agrees with the suite.

### 6.2 The disassembler

`src/disasm.rs`: Zilog's syntax with `$` for hexadecimal (`LD A,(IX-$05)`, `JR $0200`, `RST $38`), the undocumented
forms by Young's names (`SLL`, `IXH`, `SET 0,(IY-$01),B`, `IN (C)`, `OUT (C),0`), and `DB $ED,$xx` for an ED opcode
the Z80 runs as a NOP. A prefix that changes nothing is consumed with its instruction (`DD 00` is a two-byte `NOP`), as
the processor executes it. CALL and RST are references to calls; the absolute loads and stores are references to reads
and writes. **Measured:** in 1,637 opcodes the processor runs without a jump, the disassembler's length equals the
bytes the processor took; golden lines pin the syntax.

### 6.3 The state block

`State` for `Z80` (`src/state.rs`): the main, alternate and index registers, SP, PC, I, R, WZ, Q, P, IFF1, IFF2, IM,
EI's delay and HALT, 35 bytes. The layout is pinned by a test, and a short block is refused with the processor
unchanged.

### 6.4 The processor's own cost (measured 2026-10-05)

`examples/cost.rs`: a sound driver's kind of loop (a table read, arithmetic, a store, a branch, a call with an indexed
load and an OUT, DJNZ) on a flat RAM bus that only counts T-states, 50 million instructions, median of three runs, on
the desktop (Ryzen 7 7700X): **6.03 ns an instruction**, 9.6 T-states an instruction, 1,592 million emulated T-states
a second, 445 times the Genesis's 3.58 MHz. A frame's Z80 work (59,736 T-states) takes **0.038 ms**. Another job held
the load average at 6.9 throughout, so these are an upper bound. The Z80 is a small part of `Nephrite_Plan.md` §5.5's
budget.
